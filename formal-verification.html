Formal verification for AI and scientific computation

Formal verification establishes selected propositions relative to exact definitions, hypotheses, and trusted components. In machine learning and autonomous science, a proof of a specified property does not establish the correctness of the underlying empirical model, the completeness of the specification, or the fidelity of a software-to-physical-world interpretation.

Checking evidence from external tools

MathEvidence is an experimental research project connecting external mathematical computation to Lean-checked evidence. Its repository documents capability-specific checkers, exact candidate binding, and registered eligibility. Solver output and generated files are not treated as independent proof authority; the declared checker and its trusted execution path determine the precise claim established.

The project's public status and known trust gaps are essential for interpreting the results. General certification of arbitrary external mathematics is outside its stated scope.

Exact properties of machine learning models

LeanVerifier is a Lean 4 research preview for a narrowly specified property of affine binary classifiers over exact rational values. The repository explicitly marks release authorization as pending and the formal claim as under validation. This work does not establish certification of arbitrary neural networks, large language models, or production machine learning services.

Verification at the action boundary

My ACM SaT-CPS 2026 proceedings paper studies control at the final boundary between a proposed plan and a cyber-physical action. The subsequently accepted IWSEC 2026 paper concerns runtime enforcement for agentic CPS tool invocation. Their formal assumptions and empirical evaluations belong to the manuscripts, rather than to a broad claim of safe autonomous operation.

In mathematical reasoning, Feedback Aliasing in Lean Repair Loops: A Controlled Intervention Study is listed among accepted papers at the NeurIPS 2026 MATH-AI workshop, with a workshop-linked OpenReview record. The exact result scope belongs to that paper; workshop acceptance does not certify its experimental conclusions.
