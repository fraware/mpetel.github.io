AI for science: from computational capability to scientific evidence

AI for science joins machine learning, scientific computation, and laboratory practice. The central research question explored in my Forward Science essays concerns how institutions transform the output of increasingly capable tools into reproducible evidence and decisions that improve the next experiment.

The scientific loop is the object of deployment

Prediction quality and experimental throughput are useful measurements, although neither establishes whether a scientific program learns from its results. A scientific loop connects questions, representations, experimental choices, measurement, interpretation, validation, and the record retained for future decisions. Its strength depends on the correspondence between these activities, including the conditions under which a model's output justifies an intervention.

The essay The Unit of Deployment Is the Scientific Loop develops this thesis. The analysis of AI research automation separately distinguishes task automation, delegated work, permission, verification, and consequential authority.

Forward-deployed scientists

The forward-deployed scientist is proposed as a scientific role embedded in a research environment. The role combines domain judgment with software development and the translation of local experimental constraints into reusable computational tools. The proposal concerns the organization of research, including how contributions receive credit, funding, and independent scrutiny; it is an institutional argument, not an empirically established job-category standard.

Read From Extension Agents to Forward-Deployed Scientists for the historical analogy, Who Pays for the Scientist Who Makes Other Scientists Better for economic incentives, and Between the Manual Lab and the Self-Driving Lab for laboratory transitions.

Evidence and research infrastructure

Scientific agents act in environments containing incomplete records, partially automated instruments, domain-specific protocols, and consequential decisions. One research direction asks which evidence survives tool execution and how downstream actors determine whether a prior claim remains applicable. The essay Science After Text examines machine-legible knowledge, while The Experiment Has Already Changed examines the importance of retained experimental state.

LabTrust-Gym explores coordinated action and security in a simulated laboratory; it is not clinical software. MathEvidence explores narrow, explicit proof interfaces for external mathematical computation and remains an experimental research preview.
